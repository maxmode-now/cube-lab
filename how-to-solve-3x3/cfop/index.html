How to solve a 3×3 with CFOP

The standard speedcubing method: Cross, F2L, OLL, PLL. This page is the entry-level 4-look last layer — few algorithms, then practise each step in Cube Lab.

Before you start

If you cannot yet solve a 3×3, use the beginner method first. CFOP is the same cube with fewer moves once that recipe feels easy. Notation: cube notation reference.

Hold white on the bottom after the cross (unlike the beginner pages, which build white on top). Algorithms still assume a face is Front — do not turn the cube in your hands mid-algorithm.

4-look last layer, not full CFOP. Full OLL is 57 cases and full PLL is 21. Here you split OLL into two looks and PLL into two looks, so you only need a handful of algorithms.

Why CFOP

CFOP is the mainstream speed path: layers, strong lookahead, and the largest set of tutorials and case libraries. Prefer blocks, fewer rotations, and an M-slice finish? Compare with Roux (that page has a side-by-side table).

Most cubers learn CFOP after beginner because Cross and F2L reuse the same white-first intuition, then swap the slow beginner last layer for 4-look OLL/PLL.

CFOP overview

Fewer moves, faster times — four stages: Cross · F2L · OLL · PLL.

Solved cube — colour map

Cross → F2L (first two layers) → OLL (orient last layer) → PLL (permute last layer). 4-look last layer is two OLL steps plus two PLL steps.

1 · Cross — efficiently

Build the bottom white cross in about 4–8 moves by planning ahead during inspection.

White cross matching the side centres

Same white cross as the beginner method, but you plan the whole route before the timer starts. Trace all four white edges in your head: where each sits, which face turn brings it to D, and whether the side colour already matches.

A white plus that does not match the side centres is not finished — those edges will fight F2L. Prefer placing edges so their second colour lines up with the centre; daisy-on-top then flip to D is fine while learning, but aim for direct inserts as you get faster.

Most solvers then keep white on the bottom for F2L and the last layer. There is no set algorithm — inspection plus practice.

2 · F2L — pairing corner + edge

Insert the first two layers by pairing a corner and edge and placing them together.

First two layers done — white on the bottom

The beginner method inserts corners, then middle-layer edges, separately. F2L builds a corner+edge pair and drops both into a slot at once — fewer moves, and you start looking at the next pair while the current one finishes.

The basic case: the pair is split in the top layer and the slot is empty. Algorithms below assume the front-right slot. Mirror them left or right depending on where the pair sits. AUF (U turns) to set up; do not rotate the whole cube mid-insert.

Insert into the right slot
U R U' R'
Insert into the left slot
U' L' U L
Insert via the front
U' F' U F

3 · OLL ① — yellow cross

Get a yellow + on top (orient the edges). Side colours do not matter yet.

Yellow cross only

Once F2L is done, orient the top edges first. The shape goes dot → L-shape → line → cross.

Apply one algorithm until the cross appears. Hold an L with its arms left and up, and a line horizontally.

Yellow cross — repeat until formed
F R U R' U' F'

3 · OLL ② — yellow corners

Make the whole top yellow (orient the corners). 4-look uses Sune and Antisune.

Top all yellow

From the yellow cross, orient the corners. Repeat and reposition as needed. Later, seven OLL corner cases finish this in one algorithm — you do not need those yet.

It will look scrambled in between. The first two layers may break and then come back. Do not stop mid-algorithm.

Sune
R U R' U R U2 R'
Antisune

4 · PLL ① — corner positions

Move the top corners into place (3-corner cycle). The top stays yellow.

Corners in the right seats

Use A-perm to cycle three corners. If one corner is already correct, work from it. If none is, apply it once and look again. For a diagonal swap, apply it twice.

3-corner cycle (A-perm)
R' F R' B2 R F' R' B2 R2

4 · PLL ② — edge positions

Cycle the last edges. The cube is solved.

Solved

Two U-perm variants cover the 3-edge cycles. Finish with an AUF (a turn of the top) to align the sides.

Practice path

  • Cross only: scramble, inspect, solve white cross under ~8 moves; ignore the rest.
  • Add F2L with the three basic inserts; pause to name the pair before you turn.
  • Add 2-look OLL, then 2-look PLL — untimed full solves until the holds are automatic.
  • Timed solves once 4-look last layer needs no lookup. Then deepen with the F2L, OLL, and PLL libraries.

Stuck?

If a layer you already solved is a mess, you likely rotated the cube mid-algorithm. Undo to the last stage that still looks like the diagram, then retry that step in the trainer.

After this page

When 4-look last layer is comfortable, add cases from the libraries: F2L first (biggest time save), then full PLL, then full OLL. Colour neutrality on the cross (any colour as D) comes later.

FAQ

Should I learn CFOP or Roux?

CFOP is the common speed path: layers, lookahead, and huge learning resources. Roux uses fewer moves and fewer rotations, with an M-slice finish. Try both after beginner; pick the one you enjoy drilling. Side-by-side: Roux overview.

Do I need full OLL and PLL to use CFOP?

No. This page is 4-look last layer: 2-look OLL and 2-look PLL. Full 57 OLL and 21 PLL come later.

How efficient should my cross be?

Aim for about 4–8 moves planned in inspection. Colour-match every white edge to its side centre. A white plus that does not match the sides is not finished.

When should I memorise F2L algorithms?

After the three basic inserts feel automatic. Use the F2L library for cases you keep fumbling; stay intuitive for easy pairs.

What is the practice order for CFOP?

Cross under 8 moves, then intuitive F2L, then 2-look OLL, then 2-look PLL. Timed full solves come after each stage is comfortable untimed.
