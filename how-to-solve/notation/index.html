Cube notation reference

WCA-style move notation used in Cube Lab guides and algorithms. Read left to right — each token is one turn.

One page for every guide. Beginner pages link here instead of repeating the same tables. Practise turns in Cube Lab with the CTAs below.

Face turns

Each letter is a 90° clockwise turn of that face, as if you were looking straight at it.

  • U — Up (top)
  • D — Down (bottom)
  • L — Left
  • R — Right
  • F — Front
  • B — Back

On a physical cube, “clockwise” follows the face you are looking at — not always the same direction in space.

Prime and half turns

Suffixes modify the turn after the letter.

SymbolMeaningExample
' (prime)Counter-clockwise 90°R' — right face the other way
2180° (half turn)U2 — top face half turn
(none)Clockwise 90°F — front face clockwise

Read algorithms left to right: R U R' U' is four separate moves, not one long word.

How to hold the cube

Guides assume a fixed orientation unless a step says otherwise.

  • White centre on top, green centre in front — default for beginner guides.
  • Algorithms assume a face is Front. Do not rotate the whole cube in your hands mid-algorithm unless the recipe tells you to.
  • After an x / y / z rotation (below), treat the new top/front as your reference.

Where this shows up

  • 3×3 beginner and 2×2 beginner — face turns, primes, half turns.
  • CFOP and its F2L / OLL / PLL libraries — same letters; PLL H-perm uses M.
  • Roux — M, wide r / Rw, and LSE with M + U.
  • 4×4 / 5×5 — inner 2R, wide Rw, mid 3R on 5×5.

M, E, and S slices 3×3

Middle slices between opposite faces. Each turns the same way as its reference face.

MoveSliceSame direction as
MBetween L and RL
EBetween U and DD
SBetween F and BF

M' and M2 follow the same prime / half rules. Roux LSE uses M and U heavily — the M slice is the middle vertical band between left and right blocks.

Do not re-grip for M. On a physical cube, M follows L. In Cube Lab use Moves → M, or the M key (Shift = M′).

Wide turns

Turn an outer face together with the slice(s) beside it.

WCA suffix: Rw Uw Fw … — outer + adjacent slice(s). Prime and 2 work the same (Rw', Uw2).

Lowercase letter: in some Roux writing, r means the same as Rw (wide R = R + M′). Cube Lab accepts both styles in algorithm strings.

On 3×3, Rw is R plus the middle slice — useful in Roux Second Block. On 4×4+, wide usually means outer + inner slice (see below).

Big-cube slices 4×4+

Even and odd big cubes add numbered prefixes for inner and middle layers.

NotationMeaningCube Lab
2R 2U …Inner slice next to that face (where wings live on 4×4)Moves → Layer → Inner
Rw Uw …Outer + inner togetherLayer → Wide
3R 3U …Middle slice (fixed centres on 5×5)Layer → Mid 5×5
3Rw …Outer + inner + midLayer → 3-Wide 5×5

Undo wide/inner turns on big cubes. Pairing uses Uw to bring wings together, then you reverse that slice so centres return to place.

Whole-cube rotations (x, y, z)

Rotate the entire puzzle — same prime and half suffixes apply.

MoveSame axis asEffect
xRRoll cube as if doing R (right-side rotation)
yUSpin cube as if doing U (vertical axis)
zFTwist cube as if doing F (front-back axis)

Some advanced algorithms use x / y / z to avoid re-gripping. Cube Lab can play them in ▶ Demo, but there is no dedicated keyboard key — on a physical cube, rotate in your hands and re-establish white-up / green-front.

FAQ

What does a prime (') mean?

Counter-clockwise 90° looking at that face. R' turns the right face the other way from R.

What is the difference between M and x?

M turns only the middle slice between L and R. x rotates the whole cube on the same axis as R — every layer moves.

What is a wide turn?

Rw (or lowercase r in some Roux writing) turns the outer face plus the adjacent slice. On 3×3 that is R plus M′; on 4×4+ it is outer plus inner.

Where is this notation used in the guides?

Every how-to page links here. Beginner and CFOP use U–B and primes; Roux adds M and wide r; 4×4/5×5 add inner, wide, and mid layers.
